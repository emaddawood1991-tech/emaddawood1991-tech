# Medical accuracy review (author review against the cited sources — clinician sign-off REQUIRED before publication)
Sources used for the script: American Migraine Foundation (cervicogenic headache), Berkshire Healthcare NHS (cervicogenic headaches), JOSPT Neck Pain Clinical Practice Guidelines (PubMed 28666405), physiotherapy-interventions review (PubMed 35596553). The pages were not re-read inside this session's sandbox; wording follows the user's approved script and these guidance themes: cervicogenic headache is a secondary headache, distinct from migraine; assessment first; exercise/mobility as part of a graded physiotherapy plan; individual variation.

## What the video says / does NOT say
- Says: headache *may sometimes* be related to neck muscles/joints; "not every headache is caused by the neck"; assessment of neck movement, muscles and symptoms; exercises are examples within a physiotherapy plan; plans differ by case; stop a movement that increases symptoms.
- Does NOT claim: that all headaches originate in the neck, a cure, imaging for everyone, disc/nerve compression as the cause, or manipulation. No forceful manipulation or full neck rolls are shown. Migraine is not mentioned or confused.
- Pain locus and referral band are labelled/animated as a *possible pattern*, not a diagnosis; anatomy is a simplified teaching model.

## Safety warning (for the report / caption — not spoken in the 60 s video)
Sudden severe ("worst ever") headache, new neurological symptoms (weakness, numbness, speech/vision change, confusion), or headache with fever and marked neck stiffness need **urgent medical assessment**, not routine exercise advice.
