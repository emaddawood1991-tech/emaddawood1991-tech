| Statement / visual | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Scoliosis = lateral (sideways) curvature of the spine | Correct; usually with vertebral rotation; defined radiographically (Cobb angle ≥ 10°). |
| Not necessarily painful | Correct; do not depict pain. |
| Hyperkyphosis = excessive rounding of the upper back | Correct; normal thoracic kyphosis is physiological (≈ 20–45°). Must not label every rounded back as a deformity; postural kyphosis vs structural (e.g. Scheuermann) differ. |
| Early detection, especially during growth years | Consistent with screening advice (adolescent growth). |
| Adams forward-bend test | Standard screening: patient bends forward, examiner looks for a rib/loin prominence; scoliometer angle of trunk rotation. Screening, not diagnosis. |
| Imaging "when needed" | Correct; standing radiographs are used when clinically indicated, not for everyone. |
| Visual risk | Never imply a person is diagnosed from appearance; avoid exaggerated curves; no outcome promises; no unverified claim that FMC has in-house X-ray. |
